Confidential Memo: The Washington Township Incident
From: Phillip Price, CEO, ECorp
To: Internal Executive Committee
Classification: Eyes Only

Subject: The Washington Township Disaster: Admission and Ongoing Cover-Up Operations

Overview:
This document contains a full admission regarding the events surrounding the Washington Township chemical leak and the ongoing cover-up. It is meant solely for the executive leadership at ECorp. Let this serve as an internal record of our decisions, strategies, and the path forward in maintaining control of the situation.

1. Initial Incident

The Washington Township plant was, as you are aware, never meant to be publicly scrutinized. The facility served a dual purpose: a chemical manufacturing site and a covert research hub for ECorp’s more experimental and classified projects. On June 17, 1993, a chemical leak occurred in the plant’s cooling system, releasing toxic substances into the air and groundwater. This incident was the direct result of cost-cutting measures that we implemented as part of a broader strategy to meet profit goals that fiscal year.

While the official report states that the leak was contained within 24 hours, the truth is much darker. The leak continued for months, leading to increased toxicity levels that affected the surrounding population—most notably the Alderson and Moss families. Several workers and local residents began showing signs of illness soon after, many of which we were able to classify as unrelated health issues through manipulated medical reports.

2. Decision to Conceal

Upon realizing the full extent of the disaster, the Board of Directors and I made a calculated decision to cover up the incident. Exposing the truth would have been catastrophic for ECorp. We would have faced lawsuits, mass resignations, and the dissolution of our most lucrative government contracts. The financial and reputational damage was unacceptable, and so we took action.

Bribed Federal Inspectors: We ensured that federal safety inspections at Washington Township were reported as clean and in full compliance. Key figures in the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) were paid handsomely to look the other way.

Suppressed Media Coverage: Any local journalists or whistleblowers who attempted to expose the leak were met with NDAs, financial settlements, or, in certain cases, intimidation tactics. As of now, all known witnesses have been dealt with accordingly.

Forged Health Reports: Several doctors in Washington Township were enlisted to falsify medical records for affected residents, attributing illnesses to other causes. This has been critical in keeping the true source of contamination hidden.

Financial Manipulation: Through shell companies and offshore accounts, we have funneled funds to compensate families of the affected without leaving a trace of corporate involvement.

3. The Moss and Alderson Families

Special attention was given to the families of Edward Alderson and Mrs. Moss, who were both vocal early on about the health impacts their loved ones were suffering due to the leak.

Edward Alderson: His inquiries into ECorp's involvement were becoming increasingly aggressive. We kept him at bay for several years through legal distractions, but it became clear by 1995 that a more permanent solution was necessary. Our internal team ensured his untimely death, which was framed as an accident. His son, Elliot Alderson, remains a wildcard, though our surveillance indicates he is largely unaware of the full scope of ECorp's actions.

Mrs. Moss: Angela Moss's mother became the face of opposition to ECorp’s activities at Washington Township. Her legal battle with ECorp was carefully managed, but we knew it would only be a matter of time before the case drew national attention. Our intervention ensured her lawsuit failed, and she passed away under "natural causes," a narrative we successfully pushed through controlled channels.

Angela Moss herself has proven to be useful inside ECorp. Her efforts to uncover the truth can be redirected or neutralized as long as we maintain influence over her position.

4. The Plant’s True Purpose

As part of this cover-up, it’s crucial that we continue to divert attention from Project Berenstain, the classified research that was being conducted at the Washington Township plant. This experimental project, jointly funded by ECorp and the Dark Army, has far-reaching implications that we cannot allow to come to light.

The plant's leak provided an unintended distraction that has allowed us to continue Berenstain without scrutiny. Our partners at Whiterose's faction have assured us that the project remains under their protection, though we must remain vigilant.

5. Steps Forward: Ensuring the Legacy of ECorp

It is essential that the Washington Township disaster never reaches the public or regulatory agencies. Here are the immediate actions required to maintain this cover-up:

Neutralize Potential Threats: Individuals like Elliot Alderson and other local activists must be kept under surveillance. Should any of them show signs of gathering substantial evidence, they are to be dealt with swiftly. We have authorized discreet means to ensure their silence.

Control Media Narratives: Our Public Relations teams are to continue monitoring all media channels for any renewed interest in the Washington Township incident. Fake reports, distractions, and corporate narratives must be used to steer attention away from ECorp.

Contain Legal Threats: Any new lawsuits or legal inquiries related to the plant should be handled by our legal partners with deep-pocketed settlements or intimidation as needed. Federal protections remain in place, but we cannot be complacent.

Closing Remarks

What happened at Washington Township was a failure—our failure—but it has led us to a much greater cause. ECorp’s continued dominance in the financial world is the result of calculated risks, ones we must be willing to take to protect the future of our organization. The truth of Washington Township dies with us.

Remember: Our hands may be dirty, but they build the world.

Phillip Price
CEO, ECorp
